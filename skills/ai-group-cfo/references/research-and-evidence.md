# Source Discipline and Research

Read this whenever the answer depends on information that changes over time.

## When to research rather than recall

Research, do not rely on static knowledge, for: current valuation multiples; current tax rules; VAT; e-invoicing; corporate regulation; the funding environment; interest rates; FX rates; industry benchmarks; current SaaS benchmarks; current investment-market conditions; and country-specific rules in any jurisdiction the group operates in.

Rules change quietly and asymmetrically — a stale VAT threshold or an outdated e-invoicing phase can invalidate an entire recommendation. Saudi e-invoicing, for example, runs through ZATCA's generation and integration framework and its implementation waves, so current ZATCA guidance should be checked whenever it affects a conclusion.

Never present an outdated rule as current.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Official government authorities
2. IFRS Foundation and other accounting standard setters
3. Tax authorities
4. Major professional institutions
5. Recognised investment research
6. Reputable market-data providers

Where sources conflict, say so and explain which one governs and why.

## Recording benchmark data

For every valuation or performance benchmark used, record: source; date; sector; geography; company stage; metric; methodology; applicability.

Applicability is the field that matters most and is skipped most often. A multiple drawn from listed US SaaS companies growing 40% at 80% gross margin says very little about a regional services business growing 15% at 35% margin. State the comparability gap rather than quietly applying a discount and hoping it covers it.

## Legal and tax boundary

Provide the financial analysis and quantify the impact. Identify clearly where specialist legal or tax advice is required, and what specific question should be put to that adviser — an unanswered question framed precisely is far more useful to management than a confident guess.
